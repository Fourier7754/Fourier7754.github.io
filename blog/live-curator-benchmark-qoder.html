LiveCuratorBench on the Qoder Harness: Six More Models

2026-08-10
TL;DR

Six models reran LiveCuratorBench on the Qoder CLI harness. The charts below keep one entry per model—its stronger harness result—and a separate pairing view isolates the four models tested on both harnesses: the same model can swing by up to 2.7 points across harnesses. Qwen 3.8 Max leads the Qoder round at 18.67/22; DeepSeek v4 Flash is the best value on either harness.

I reran LiveCuratorBench on a second harness. The first thirteen-model round ran the Curator agent on my opencode fork; this round ports the same agent—main loop, reasoner subagent, stage/review/commit tool layer—to Qoder CLI CN (qoderclicn 1.1.17), running headless. The task, the source document, the 22-point rubric, and the scoring are unchanged; the harness underneath is not. The charts below keep one entry per model—its stronger harness result—opencode in blue, Qoder in green; a third chart pairs the four models that ran on both harnesses, so the same model's shift is visible directly. These shifts are descriptive, not a controlled experiment: the two harnesses differ in tool layer, permission model, and default parameters, and each cell is three runs.

Each model ran three independent times per harness with vendor-default settings. Two measurement caveats. First, the Qoder backend does not report token counts at all—every usage field in the session logs reads zero—so Qoder-side costs are metered in Qoder credits, the platform's billing unit, converted at the price I actually paid for the subscription.1 Second, the two harnesses therefore meter cost differently (tokens on opencode, credits on Qoder): cross-harness cost reads are directional, not exact. The four models that ran on both harnesses are paired in a dedicated scatter further below.2

What the results show

The same model can land in a different tier on a different harness. Four models ran on both. Qwen 3.7 Plus is the big mover: 15.33 on opencode, 18.00 on Qoder—a 2.67-point jump from the bottom half to a tie for second. Moving the other way, DeepSeek v4 Flash drops 1.33 (19.33 official to 18.00), GLM-5.2 drops 0.67 (18.67 to 18.00), and Kimi K2.7 slips 1.00 (16.33 to 15.33 for the K2.7 Code variant). A 0.67-point move is inside single-run noise; the other three are not.

The big mover's failure mode changed, not just its score. On opencode, Qwen 3.7 Plus under-extracted and swung hard—its meta-knowledge count went 9/2/5 across the three runs, and one run collapsed to 14. On Qoder it extracted fully (18/17/19) and mostly held the two items that separate this round's front row: keeping the user-message anchor as a standalone entry (G6) and extracting the always-on inline rule (G7). Same model, same task, same rubric; the harness swap turned an erratic weak run into a stable front-row one.

Model traits persist across harnesses even when scores move. GLM-5.2 and DeepSeek v4 Flash were the zero-variance models on opencode and stayed zero-variance on Qoder (18/18/18 each). Kimi K2.7 was volatile on opencode (15–17) and stayed volatile on Qoder (13–19, including one clean 19). Stability travels with the model; absolute score travels with the harness.

The two hard probes did not move at all. G5—recognizing that the input itself is a pollution source—stuck at 1 of 2 points in all 18 Qoder runs, replicating the opencode ceiling where full marks were rare and clustered. B1—"line numbers reduce hallucination" as explicit knowledge—scored 0 in all 18 runs, matching 39 out of 39 zeros on opencode. Whatever the harness changed for mid-field models, it did not touch these two items.

Qoder's two exclusive models bracket the round. Qwen 3.8 Max takes first place at 18.67 (18–19), the only model that held both G6 and G7 across all three runs; at 69.9 credits (≈$0.259) it is also the round's most expensive. Qwen 3.6 Flash trails at 13.00 with the round's only single-run collapse—its third run produced zero meta-knowledge entries and scored 6.

Every shared model got cheaper per run on Qoder—directionally. Qwen 3.7 Plus moved from $0.068 to $0.027, DeepSeek v4 Flash from $0.027 to $0.018, GLM-5.2 from $0.240 to $0.177, Kimi K2.7 from $0.222 to $0.159. The two metering systems are not the same, so read these as direction, not exact savings. One harness-specific behavior: two Qoder runs (Qwen 3.8 Max R3, Kimi K2.7 Code R3) staged their bundle twice—an intermediate state, then a completed re-stage—which the opencode round never produced.

Task definition, evaluation criteria, and the first thirteen-model results are on the LiveCuratorBench main page.
